Why Public Speaking Will Matter More Than Ever in the Age of AI

By Wade Paterson

In a world where artificial intelligence is rapidly consuming digital communication, one ancient skill is quietly becoming more valuable than ever: the ability to stand in front of other humans and speak with clarity, conviction, and authenticity. While many people are asking what AI will make obsolete, a far more interesting question is this — what existing skills will become more important as AI becomes ubiquitous? Public speaking and in-person communication sit right at the top of that list.

A Serendipitous Inheritance

Like many people, I'm always fascinated by what others choose to display in their Zoom backgrounds — the items they've deemed important enough to represent them on camera. One of the items behind me on my calls is a set of books: The Complete Speakers and Toastmasters Library. And they come with a story.

Several years ago, long before I had any interest in public speaking, my grandpa passed away. Our family gathered at his house afterward, where his belongings were laid out in the basement, almost like a garage sale. Each of us was given a different color of sticker to place on the items we wanted. I don't know what drew me to those books — I had zero interest in public speaking at the time — but I put my sticker on them without hesitation.

For years, they sat on my shelf as little more than decoration. But looking back, it's remarkable how my life naturally took a course toward joining Toastmasters and becoming deeply passionate about public speaking. Those books were always there, waiting.

Easy to Conceive, Challenging to Deliver

Recently, I cracked open one of those books — Speech Openers and Closers — and a single line stopped me in my tracks:

"Speeches are like giving birth. Easy to conceive, challenging to deliver."

What a brilliant piece of historic wisdom. And what makes it even more powerful is how perfectly it maps onto a very modern challenge. Yes, AI can help us conceive a speech. It can build a structure, suggest ideas, pull in facts and concepts. But when you walk in front of a crowd and it's just you — that's the real test. No algorithm can take that stage for you.

When Bots Are Talking to Bots

Digital communication, especially written text, is being rapidly swallowed up by AI. If you've noticed a shift in the emails landing in your inbox, you're not alone. The telltale signs are everywhere:

  • A colleague who never used emojis is suddenly littering them throughout their messages.
  • Someone who never touched an em dash is now using them in every sentence.
  • The font is different. The structure feels templated. The tone is unmistakably ChatGPT.

I understand that writing is challenging for some people, and AI tools genuinely help them become more efficient. But here's where it gets frustrating: people are generating lengthy, AI-written emails and sending them with the expectation that I will invest the time to read, process, and respond to all of that content — content that the sender themselves barely wrote beyond a few prompts.

So what did I start doing? I began using my AI tools to read their AI-generated emails and draft responses on my behalf. And then it hit me: it's just bots communicating with bots. Sure, I invest time to ensure my AI understands my vision, values, and communication style. But when you take a step back, the humans have largely left the conversation.

AI Slop Is Flooding Every Channel

This phenomenon isn't limited to email. It's reshaping video content too. YouTube recently tightened the rules for its Creator Partnership Program, making it significantly harder for new creators to qualify. Much of the speculation around those changes points to the explosion of what's being called "AI slop" — AI-generated videos and channels churning out content at a pace and volume that earns significant revenue through sheer output, regardless of quality.

Whether those rule changes will solve the problem remains to be seen. In my view, they probably won't do much. But the underlying trend is undeniable: AI-generated content is only going to become more common across every medium.

The Human Advantage: Delivery

This is precisely why in-person communication and public speaking are becoming more valuable, not less. Consider what AI cannot do right now:

  • It can't walk onto a stage and connect with an audience through eye contact, energy, and presence.
  • It can't listen deeply in a one-on-one conversation and respond with genuine empathy and insight.
  • It can't read a room, adjust its tone, and inspire people to take action in real time.

You could stand at a podium and read everything an AI tool has generated for you, but that's not engaging. That won't win over an audience. The magic lies in delivery — in your ability to internalize information and present it in a way that demonstrates you truly understand it and care about it. That's an irreplaceably human skill.

The Coming Reckoning

Here's the reality that few people are talking about: those who have been hiding behind their keyboards, letting AI do the heavy lifting in their communication, will eventually be exposed. The moment they're in a room, on a stage, or in a meeting where they're asked to think on their feet — to leverage impromptu speaking, to showcase genuine understanding, to inspire — the gap between AI-assisted personas and authentic communicators will become glaringly obvious.

People are going to be caught out. And those who have invested in sharpening their public speaking and interpersonal communication skills will stand apart in ways that matter enormously.

Now Is the Time to Invest in Your Voice

If you've been thinking about working on your public speaking skills, I implore you — do it now. Join a Toastmasters club. Volunteer to present at your next team meeting. Say yes to the speaking opportunity that makes your palms sweat. The ability to communicate powerfully in person has always been valuable, but AI's rise is making it even more so.

The age-old wisdom still holds true today: speeches may be easy to conceive, but they are challenging to deliver. And in a world where machines are handling more and more of the conceiving, the humans who can masterfully deliver — who can stand before others and communicate with authenticity, skill, and heart — will be the ones who truly stand out. That's not a skill AI is replacing. It's a skill AI is making indispensable.
