5 Virtual Meeting Mistakes That Make You Look Unprofessional (And How to Fix Them)

By Wade Paterson

Public speaking has long been recognised as the most common personal fear in the world — and in recent years, it has taken on an entirely new form. Virtual meetings and presentations are now a staple of professional life, yet many people unknowingly sabotage their credibility every time they log on. The good news? Most of these pitfalls are easy to fix. Here are five common virtual meeting mistakes and the practical steps you can take to ensure you come across as polished, engaging, and professional on every call.

1. Getting Your Lighting Wrong

Lighting is one of the most common — and most overlooked — mistakes on virtual calls. Two scenarios cause the most problems:

  • A window behind you: When sunlight or daylight streams in from behind, it creates a silhouette effect. Your face and body appear dark on camera, and while some webcams try to compensate, the result is never flattering.
  • Overhead lighting only: A ceiling light directly above you casts harsh, unflattering shadows across your face — the kind that make you look tired at best and slightly ominous at worst.

The solution is simple: position your light source in front of you, shining directly onto your face. Two umbrella lights or softbox lights placed on either side of your camera will give you an even, professional look. While ring lights are popular, a two-light setup with diffusers tends to produce a more natural, polished result. Whatever you choose, the principle remains the same — light should be coming towards you, not from behind or above.

2. Failing to Make Eye Contact with the Camera

This is a subtle mistake that makes a dramatic difference. Most people position their webcam at the top of their screen, and then spend the entire meeting looking at the faces displayed below it. While it feels natural to watch your colleagues' reactions, doing so means you're not actually making eye contact with anyone.

When you look directly at your camera lens, you simultaneously make eye contact with every single person on the call. That connection creates accountability, makes your message more engaging, and gives you a commanding presence.

This doesn't mean you should stare unblinkingly at the camera the entire time. Glance down periodically to gauge reactions and read the room. But make a conscious effort to return your gaze to the lens — especially when you're making an important point. Whether you're presenting to a boardroom or catching up with your team, this one adjustment will instantly elevate how you come across.

3. Hiding Your Hand Gestures

Hand gestures are a powerful communication tool, but the small frame of a virtual meeting window makes them easy to lose. Many people gesture naturally while they speak — but their hands stay below the frame, leaving the audience with nothing more than an occasional flash of a thumb or fingertip.

The fix is to be intentional about bringing your gestures up into the visible frame. Let your audience see the full movement, and allow it to reinforce what you're saying. A word of caution, though: forced or exaggerated gestures can feel awkward and unnatural. The goal isn't to wave your arms around like you're directing traffic. Instead, use the same natural hand movements you'd use in an in-person conversation — just lift them into view so the camera can capture them.

4. Sitting Down When You Could Be Standing

Here's a pro tip that not everyone considers: try standing during your virtual calls. Think about the difference between watching a dynamic keynote speaker command a stage versus a seated panel discussion. Both might deliver great content, but the energy is noticeably different. The same principle applies to virtual meetings.

When you stand, your body shifts into a more active mode. Your posture improves, your breathing deepens, and you naturally project more energy and enthusiasm. If you have a sit-stand desk, this is easy to implement. Even if you don't, a stack of books or a sturdy box can elevate your laptop to the right height.

A practical approach is to sit for focused, solo work — answering emails, editing documents, handling administrative tasks — and stand whenever you're communicating, whether that's a team meeting, a client presentation, or a content recording session. Many people who adopt this habit describe it as a genuine game-changer for both their energy levels and the quality of their interactions.

5. Skipping the Tech Check

We all know the classic blunder — "You're on mute!" — but failing to test your technology goes far beyond forgetting to unmute. Before any important virtual meeting, run through the following:

  • Microphone selection: Make sure the correct microphone is active. The difference between a dedicated external microphone and your laptop's built-in mic is significant. Your audience will notice.
  • Speaker setup: Confirm that sound is coming through the right output so you're not scrambling to hear people when the meeting begins.
  • Screen sharing: If you plan to share your screen, practise beforehand. Know how to navigate between windows, and learn how to share audio alongside your screen if you'll be playing a video or demonstration.
  • Mute discipline: Know where your mute button is, keep yourself muted when you're not speaking, and remember to unmute before you start talking.

A quick five-minute practice run before any high-stakes meeting eliminates those awkward technical hiccups and allows you to focus entirely on your message. It's one of the simplest things you can do to appear more professional and prepared.

Put It All Together and Stand Out

What's remarkable about these five mistakes is how many experienced professionals — people who have been on video calls for years — still make them regularly. The bar, in other words, is not as high as you might think. By investing in proper front-facing lighting, making deliberate eye contact with your camera, bringing your hand gestures into frame, standing up to boost your energy, and testing your technology before every important call, you will immediately distinguish yourself as one of the most polished and engaging communicators in any virtual room you enter. These are small, practical adjustments — but together, they make an extraordinary difference.
